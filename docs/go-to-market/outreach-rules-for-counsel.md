# Outreach rules: a briefing for the owner's lawyer

Drafted 2026-11-25 for the founder of Rail to hand to counsel.

**This is not legal advice and it states no legal conclusion.** It was written by a software assistant, not a lawyer. Every rule below is a summary of a public page as it was read through a summarising tool on 2026-11-25. Nothing here says that any activity is legal, illegal, or exempt. Every summary must be confirmed with counsel against the primary text. Where a page could not be read in full it is marked UNVERIFIED and the gap is stated.

## 1. What Rail wants to do

- **Who:** one founder, based in the Cincinnati / Northern Kentucky area, selling business software (payroll, tips, sales and stock reporting for a bar) to the owners or managers of independent, licensed bars. Business to business. The software reads data from the bar's 2Touch point-of-sale system.
- **Where:** bars in Ohio and Kentucky. The metro area crosses the state line, so both states matter.
- **Live now (lowest risk, chosen in the strategy):** in-person visits at quiet hours, warm introductions from bar owners the founder knows, inbound interest (a demo-request form on the website, people who ask).
- **On hold until counsel answers:** cold phone calls, calls to owners' mobile phones, any text message to a prospect who has not asked for one (including hand-typed), cold email, direct messages on social media.
- **Not decided:** whether supplier reps (liquor and beer distributor reps who visit bars) are asked for introductions, and whether anything is given back to them. Nothing is offered today.
- **Entity status:** no legal entity, domain or postal address is on record yet. See section 6.
- Source of the plan: `docs/go-to-market/strategy.md`, section 5.

### 2.2 Cold phone calls to a business line (federal)

- The FTC page says: "Most phone calls between a telemarketer and a business are exempt from the TSR." It lists exceptions, including calls "to induce the retail sale of nondurable office or cleaning supplies," and calls to business lines that "solicit individual employees to buy products or services for their own use."
- On the National Do Not Call Registry the page says (second half of page): "The prohibition on calls to numbers on the Registry does not apply to business-to-business calls." It also says consumers may register landline and wireless numbers.
- Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-telemarketing-sales-rule , read 2026-11-25.
- **UNVERIFIED:** the page is about 203,000 characters; the tool showed characters 0 to 200,000. ### 2.3 Calls to a business owner's cell phone, and the TCPA

- 47 CFR 64.1200, as summarised: calls using an autodialer or artificial or prerecorded voice are barred unless for emergencies or with "prior express consent" (a)(1); advertising or telemarketing calls of that kind to wireless numbers need "prior express written consent" (a)(2), defined at (f)(9) as a signed agreement that clearly authorises the seller and states that signing is not a condition of purchase; a called party may revoke consent by "any reasonable method," honoured within a reasonable time not exceeding ten business days (a)(10); do-not-call requests must be honoured within ten business days and for 5 years (d)(3), (d)(6); registry rules (c)(2) refer to "residential" subscribers.
- Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/64.1200 , read 2026-11-25 (Cornell's copy, not the eCFR; it may lag the official text). The tool said the text appeared complete through paragraph (s).
- **Unclear:** the page does not say whether a business's cell phone, or a personal cell used for business, is "residential". It does not define whether a hand-typed text to one number uses an "automatic telephone dialing system".
- **UNVERIFIED:** the FCC's own TCPA consumer guide returned HTTP 403 on an earlier attempt and was not retried. The FTC page said the FCC "enforces the TCPA", and the TSR page did not address texts. Case law on what counts as an autodialer was not read at all.

### 2.4 Text messages (including a hand-typed one)

### 2.5 Email

- FTC CAN-SPAM guide: "The law makes no exception for business-to-business email." Requirements as listed on the page: accurate header information; non-deceptive subject lines; clear disclosure that the message is an advertisement; your valid physical postal address (a street address, a USPS-registered post office box, or a private mailbox registered with a commercial mail receiving agency are listed as acceptable); a clear way to opt out; honour an opt-out "within 10 business days"; the opt-out mechanism must work for at least 30 days after sending; you remain responsible for email others send for you. The page states "Each separate email in violation of the CAN-SPAM Act is subject to penalties of up to $53,088" (page notes a January 2024 inflation adjustment).
- Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business , read 2026-11-25. The tool said the whole page was seen. This is the best-read source here.
- **Unclear:** whether a one-to-one, individually written first message to a business address counts as a "commercial message" under the Act, and what the unsubscribe mechanism must look like for a founder sending from a personal mailbox.

### 2.9 Ohio telephone solicitor law (R.C. Chapter 4719)

- R.C. 4719.01(A)(8) defines "telephone solicitor" as "a person that engages in telephone solicitation directly or through one or more salespersons", either "from a location in this state, or from a location outside this state to persons in this state."
- R.C. 4719.01(B) lists 27 exemptions from sections 4719.02 to 4719.18 and 4719.99. The business-to-business exemption (B)(12), as summarised, has four alternative conditions: (a) operating continuously for at least three years under the same business name and at least 51 per cent of gross dollar volume from repeat sales to existing customers; (b) the purchaser intends to resell the goods; (c) the purchaser intends to use the goods or services in a recycling, reuse, manufacturing or remanufacturing process; (d) a publisher of a periodical soliciting related advertising or subscriptions. Other exemptions that counsel may want to look at for a new company: (B)(1) one-time or infrequent transactions "not done in the course of a pattern of repeated transactions"; (B)(18) established retail businesses (same name at least one year); (B)(25) repeat sales to previous purchasers (three years under the same name).
- R.C. 4719.02(A), as summarised: "No person shall act as a telephone solicitor without first having obtained a certificate of registration" from the attorney general. The application asks for identity, business history, criminal and civil history, bankruptcy, banks, and sales scripts; changes must be notified within thirty days; a non-resident must keep an Ohio resident as statutory agent. The tool saw **no bond requirement** in 4719.02. The page lists a December 5, 1996 version as the one shown.
- Sources: https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-4719.01 and https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-4719.02 , read 2026-11-25. The tool said each page appeared complete.
- **UNVERIFIED:** 4719.03 to 4719.08 were not read, so any bond or fee requirement elsewhere in the chapter, and any penalty, is unknown. Whether the 1996 version shown for 4719.02 is current is unknown. The Ohio Attorney General's own telemarketing page was not read (earlier attempt showed only a list of registrants). Whether the exemption conditions are met by a new company is the question, not an answer.

### 2.10 Kentucky telemarketing law (KRS 367.46951 and following)

- Kentucky Attorney General page "About Kentucky's No Call Law" (tool summary): a "telemarketing company" or "merchant" making a "telephone solicitation" must obtain the No Call list. Telemarketing company: "a company whose primary business is to engage in telephone solicitation"; must register and file "a surety bond in the amount of $50,000.00" at least ten days before doing business in Kentucky, plus a $300 fee (annual renewal $50). Merchant: "the individual or business entity offering consumer goods or services" (also investment, business or employment opportunities, consumer loans); the page lists no registration or bond for a merchant. The page describes the law as covering calls to "residential or wireless telephone numbers on the Kentucky No Call list". It says some calls are excluded from "solicitation", including "A telephone call made by one merchant to another" and calls where there is a prior or existing business relationship. It also says a person using automated calling equipment must file an application and a "$10,000 surety bond" before calling Kentucky numbers, unless an exception applies.
- Source: https://ag.ky.gov/Resources/Consumer-Resources/Consumers/Pages/Information-for-Telemarketers.aspx , read 2026-11-25. The tool said the page appeared complete but it did not follow the linked statutes.
- **UNVERIFIED:** the statute text of KRS 367.46951 itself was **not read**. One legislature URL returned an unrelated "Invalid Statute" notice; another returned an unreadable PDF. A search confirmed the definitions section exists (it names "telephone solicitation", "merchant", "telemarketer", "telemarketing company", "zero call list") but the current wording was not seen. The Attorney General's page is therefore a summary of the law, not the law. Kentucky's regulations (a search surfaced 40 KAR 2:001) were not read.

## 3. Questions for counsel

Answerable yes/no or in a line. Numbers 1 to 5 are the manager's required starting list.

1. Does Ohio R.C. 4719.02 require Rail (a new company with no history) to register with the Ohio Attorney General, or post a bond, before it phones Ohio bars? Which R.C. 4719.01(B) exemption, if any, can a company with no three-year history and no repeat-sales record rely on? Is a selling-software call a "sale of goods or services" within the chapter at all?
2. Is a call, or a hand-typed text, to a bar owner's personal mobile phone treated as a consumer contact (residential subscriber, TCPA consumer rules, registry rules) when the number is the one the owner uses for the bar?
3. May Rail pay or reward a supplier rep or distributor for an introduction to a bar owner? If not money, may it give anything of value (a free month, a meal, a mention)? Which Ohio, Kentucky and federal alcohol-trade rules apply to that?
4. If the founder meets an owner in person and the sale is completed at that meeting, does R.C. 4719.01(B)(3) (face-to-face completion) or any other exemption change the answer to Q1 for a follow-up call to arrange the visit?
5. What must a consent line say for a prospect who asks to be texted? What proof of it must be kept, and for how long?
6. May the 2Touch agent read the POS database under the bar's licence from 2Touch? (This is a contract question, not an outreach one; it is listed in the strategy and is needed before approaching 2Touch.)
7. Do Kentucky's rules apply to Rail's calls to Kentucky bars? Is Rail a "merchant" or a "telemarketing company", and does a merchant-to-merchant call fall outside the Kentucky "telephone solicitation" definition in the current statute text?
8. If Rail is not a "telemarketing company" in Kentucky, does it still need the No Call list for any call to a Kentucky number, even a business line? Does the answer change when a bar owner answers on a mobile?
9. Is a business's own cell number covered by the TCPA when used for business, and does a sole proprietor's personal mobile count as business use?
10. Is a founder hand-typing one text, one number at a time from a phone, an "automatic telephone dialing system" use? Does it change if the text is sent from a web tool, a CRM or Rail's SMS provider?
11. Do federal (TTB), Ohio or Kentucky alcohol rules limit how Rail may contact a liquor licensee or obtain licensee contact details (for example from public permit lookups)?
12. May Rail accept an introduction from a distributor's rep if nothing is given back? Does a rep's employer's policy or the rep's own licence rules need to be considered?
13. What must be in a first cold email to a bar's business address: postal address, an unsubscribe link or a reply-to-opt-out line, an "advertisement" label? Does a one-to-one individually written first email still count as commercial?
14. When must a legal entity and a domain exist before Rail sends email or invoices (sender identity, postal address, mailbox on a business domain)? Is a registered agent address or a PO box acceptable as the postal address?
15. What must an opt-out do (channel-by-channel): which words count, how fast must it be honoured, how long must the record be kept, and does an opt-out on one channel bind another?
16. What must Rail keep as records of every contact (date, channel, source of the number, consent wording, opt-outs), for how long, and where?
17. May the founder use public permit or liquor-licence listings to build a list of bars, and does any rule limit use of that data?
18. Does the website demo-request form need specific consent wording or a privacy notice to permit a reply by call, text or email? Does the existing privacy page cover it?
19. Is a social-media direct message to a bar's business account treated as commercial email, a text, or neither?
20. Do any city or county rules (Cincinnati, Covington, Newport, Hamilton, Kenton, Campbell counties) limit in-person solicitation at licensed premises?
21. Does the answer differ if a person other than the founder (a contractor, a friend) makes the contact on Rail's behalf?
22. Penalties: what are the actual current penalties for each channel? (No penalty figure from a primary page is relied on here except the CAN-SPAM figure quoted above.)

Count: 22 questions.

## 4. What the founder will do meanwhile, and what waits

### 4.1 Allowed to do today (the strategy's lowest-risk channels)

- Visit in person at quiet hours (before service). Introduce yourself, leave a printed card or note, ask to come back. Do not demand to see staff or keep pressing after a "no".
- Ask bar owners the founder already knows for a warm introduction to another bar owner. Unpaid. Nothing offered in return.
- Answer people who contact Rail first: the demo-request form, a reply, a direct question. Reply on the channel they used. If someone asks to be texted, get their request in writing (a text or note from them) and keep it.
- Keep a simple log: date, who (a role or a number, not a stranger's name beyond what is needed), channel, how they were found, what was said, any "stop" request.
- If anyone says stop, stop on every channel and write it down.

### 4.2 Do not do until counsel answers

- No cold phone calls to any bar, business line or mobile.
- No texts to anyone who has not asked for one, including hand-typed ones. Never use Rail's product SMS number for prospecting.
- No cold email. No email at all until a domain, mailbox and postal address exist (the strategy says nothing printed or emailed goes out before then).
- No unsolicited direct messages on social media.
- No payment, gift, discount or favour to a supplier rep or distributor for an introduction.
- No bought or scraped lead lists.
- No collecting personal mobile numbers from lists or from the web.
- No claim that Rail is "exempt", "registered" or "compliant". Nobody has said so.
